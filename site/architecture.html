Architecture

Six planes, enforced at two independent points

The system separates who you are from how much you are trusted from what you are permitted to do right now. Conflating those is how agent systems end up with a single point of failure.

Structure

The six planes

PlaneAnswersBuilt onState
Communication Can these two agents reach each other at all? SAM, libp2p, Biscuit, MCP upstream
Identity Who is this agent, and does it really control that PeerID? ERC-8004 + the Agent Mesh Binding built
Trust How much should I rely on it? ERC-8004 reputation & validation specified
Compliance Is it permitted to do this specific thing, right now? ERC-8226 / RAMS mandates specified
Settlement Did value actually move, and can that be proven? Escrow in a test stablecoin specified
Audit What happened, and who can verify it later? Hash-chained evidence, L1 anchoring specified

Why identity and authority are kept separate

Trust tier and mandate are two independent axes. An agent can be highly reputable (T3) and hold no authority to move a particular asset; another can hold a valid mandate and still be too new to be trusted with a large job. Collapsing them into one score would make both meaningless.

Defence

Two-sided enforcement

Every consequential decision is checked at two independent points, chosen so that compromising one does not defeat the other.

Point one · admission

Can this peer be on the mesh at all?

The broker gates enrollment on a verified binding, and the control plane attests labels into the peer’s Biscuit. SAM’s own node-level Datalog then enforces those labels at the transport handshake — verified in spike S-2.

Point two · use

May this specific call proceed?

The sidecar checks the attestation, the tier, and the mandate. But the authoritative check for anything moving an asset is on-chain: the asset’s own transfer hook validates the mandate atomically with the transfer.

Why the on-chain check is the one that counts

A compromised broker or sidecar can admit a peer it should not, or approve a call it should not. Neither can move an asset, because the asset contract re-evaluates authority itself and reverts. That is what keeps broker compromise a denial-of-service and evidence-integrity problem rather than a loss-of-funds problem.

Decisions

The choices that shaped everything else

ADR-0001

No fork of SAM

Integration is via documented HTTP endpoints and configuration only. This constrains the design — and it is why a discovered upstream defect was reported rather than patched around in the product.

ADR-0003

agentWallet is the RAMS agent address

Reusing one address as both the ERC-8004 wallet and the ERC-8226 agent is what makes selling an agent revoke its authority as well as its mesh access, with no extra machinery.

ADR-0005

Two-sided enforcement

Admission and use are checked independently, and the authoritative check for asset movement is on-chain.

ADR-0004

Foundry with vendored registries

ERC-8004 contracts are vendored at a pinned commit with CI enforcing byte-identity, so an upstream change cannot silently alter what was tested.
